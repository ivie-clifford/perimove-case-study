# Survey summary: aggregate counts

**Evidence type.** Stated interest and self-report from a **self-selected sample**. This is discovery evidence only. It does not support health, adherence, beginner-suitability or product-market-fit claims.

**What this file contains.** Aggregate counts only, with the number of people who answered each question. It contains no respondent records, identifiers, contact details, timestamps or quoted comments.

**How it was run.** An online survey open for about a week in October 2026, closed with **40 responses**. The counts were checked against the exported responses before being recorded here.

## Sampling limitations
- Recruitment was a convenience sample: a hackathon community Slack, friends and Instagram. It is not representative of women in perimenopause generally.
- Multi-select and "select up to three" questions add up to more than the number of respondents. Do not read them as the share of people who chose a single option.
- Some "other" write-ins were typed into option fields; counts treat them as written.
- Interest in trying a prototype is stated interest, not behavior.
- Subgroups are small. Group comparisons are descriptive and exploratory.
- Free-text answers were read by one person and not independently coded, so they are described as themes, not counts.

**Low confidence (not very or not at all): 20 of 40.**

By relationship with exercise (the two groups of one person each are not shown):

| Group | Low confidence | Somewhat | Very | Haven't needed to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Established routine (n = 20) | 6 | 9 | 4 | 1 |
| Occasional or building (n = 18) | 13 | 4 | 1 | 0 |

Reading: low confidence was more common among people still building a routine (13 of 18) than among those with an established one (6 of 20). The groups are small and self-selected, only one respondent was new to exercise, and the survey says nothing about beginner suitability. This is a pattern worth testing, not a result about all exercisers.

Among the 20 low-confidence respondents, **15 chose rest or skip**. Resting can be the right call. The survey cannot say whether a different option was available or wanted, so this is not a finding that people skip too much.

## Free-text themes (a reading, not counts)
Recurring themes across the written answers included: wanting to tell perimenopause apart from an ordinary off day; guidance on form and joint-friendly modification; time, consistency and motivation; permission to rest; and bone and muscle health goals. A few respondents mentioned weight-loss or body-shaping goals, which are outside the prototype's scope. These are discovery signals and are not measured prevalence.
